# Drift detection: how fast the alarm fires

Generated by `monitoring/simulate_drift.py`. Do not edit by hand.

Categories: bottle, cable, capsule, carpet, grid, hazelnut, leather, metal_nut, pill, screw, tile, toothbrush, transistor, wood, zipper.

Each run streams 150 normal images, then 150 with the condition applied (one in ten is a defect). The alarm rule looks at the latest 50 images and compares them with a reference: 300 images from a normal stretch of the same kind of stream, none of which appear in the stream itself. 5 runs per category and condition.

The alarm fires when at least 4 of 19 watched numbers (brightness, contrast, sharpness and 16 embedding components) have shifted, each by a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test at p below 0.001. How that strictness was chosen: `reports/drift_calibration.md`.

- **Detection delay**: drifted images that went by before the alarm first fired.
- **Good parts failed**: share of good test images the model calls defects.
- **Defects missed**: share of defective test images the model lets through.

**False alarms before any change: 18 of 42,420 windows (0.04%), across 14 categories.**

Left out of the false-alarm and delay figures: toothbrush (18%). There the alarm goes off on clean streams more than 5% of the time, because the category has too few images for this experiment (see `reports/drift_calibration.md`), so its delays mean nothing. Its rows are still shown in the per-category tables.

## MVTec's test photos already differ from its training photos

Before any drift is simulated, the same test is applied to the good training photos against the good test photos of each category: how many of the 19 watched numbers differ (p below 0.001)? For comparison, the same test between two random halves of the training photos.

A count of 4 or more would raise the alarm. So a reference built from training photos alone would alarm on normal test photos wherever the first column shows 4 or more. That is why the reference above is a normal stretch of the stream, and why a live monitor should take its reference from a normal stretch of the live stream, not from the training set.
